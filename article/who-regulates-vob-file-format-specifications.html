Who Regulates VOB File Format Specifications?

The VOB (Video Object) format is the core container format used for storing multimedia content on DVD-Video discs. This article explains which organization governs the official technical specifications of VOB files, the history of this regulatory body, how the format is defined, and how these standards are maintained and licensed in the digital media industry.

The official specifications for the VOB file format are governed and regulated by the DVD Forum.

The DVD Forum

Originally founded in 1995 as the DVD Consortium, the organization was later renamed the DVD Forum in 1997. It is an international association composed of hardware manufacturers, software developers, content providers, and other media companies. Founding members included major technology and entertainment giants such as Toshiba, Sony, Philips, Panasonic (Matsushita), and Warner Bros. The primary purpose of the DVD Forum was to establish single, unified standards for optical disc media to avoid a costly format war similar to the earlier VHS and Betamax dispute.

How VOB Is Defined and Standardized

VOB files do not exist as an independent, standalone standard created in isolation. Instead, they are defined as an integral component of the DVD-Video Format Specification, formally cataloged within the DVD Specifications for Read-Only Disc (Part 3: DVD-Video Specification).

Technically, a VOB file is based heavily on the MPEG-2 Program Stream standard (ISO/IEC 13818-1). However, the DVD Forum established strict proprietary limitations and extensions that differentiate a generic MPEG-2 file from a compliant VOB file. These specifications regulate:

  • Multiplexed Streams: How video (MPEG-1 or MPEG-2), audio (AC-3, LPCM, DTS, or MPEG audio), and subtitles are packaged together.
  • Navigation and Control Data: The inclusion of private data streams, such as Navigation Packs (NV_PCK), which manage playback branching, search tables, and multi-angle viewing.
  • Sub-pictures: Proprietary 2-bit overlay graphics used for DVD subtitles and menu highlights.
  • Bitrate Limits: Strict bounds on maximum data transfer rates (up to 10.08 Mbps total for audio, video, and overhead) to guarantee continuous playback on hardware players.

Licensing and Enforcement

The DVD Forum oversees the technical evolution of the standard, while the licensing and enforcement of DVD formats are managed by related entities:

  • DVD Format/Logo Licensing Corporation (DVD FLLC): Handles the official licensing of the format specifications and the use of the DVD logos on hardware, software, and discs.
  • DVD Copy Control Association (DVD CCA): Manages the licensing of the Content Scramble System (CSS), the encryption scheme often applied directly to VOB streams on commercial discs.

Because the DVD Forum holds the proprietary rights to the DVD-Video standard, access to the full, official VOB technical specification books requires signing non-disclosure agreements and paying licensing fees directly to the governing organization.
