Maximum Video Bitrate for a VOB Stream

The maximum allowable peak video bitrate for a single video stream inside a standard DVD-Video VOB (Video Object) container is 9.8 Mbps (9,800 kbps). This article breaks down the official DVD specification limits for MPEG-2 video streams, explains the difference between video bitrate and total multiplex bitrate, and outlines safe authoring practices to avoid playback buffer errors.

The Standard DVD-Video Bitrate Limits

According to the official DVD-Video specification (DVD Forum), the bitrate limits within a VOB container are strictly defined to ensure hardware decoders and drive mechanisms can read and process the data reliably in real time:

  • Maximum Peak Video Bitrate: 9.8 Mbps (9,800 kbps) for a single MPEG-1 or MPEG-2 video stream.
  • Maximum Peak Multiplex (Total) Bitrate: 10.08 Mbps (10,080 kbps) for the combined system stream.

Understanding the Multiplex Overhead

While the video stream itself can legally peak at 9.8 Mbps, it can only do so if all other multiplexed elements fit within the remaining 280 kbps (0.28 Mbps) headroom. The total multiplex limit of 10.08 Mbps must accommodate:

  • The primary video stream
  • All concurrent audio streams (e.g., AC-3, DTS, or uncompressed LPCM)
  • Subtitle and subpicture streams
  • DVD packet headers and navigational overhead

If an uncompressed stereo LPCM audio track is used (which runs at 1.536 Mbps), the peak video bitrate must be lowered significantly to prevent the total stream from exceeding the 10.08 Mbps ceiling.

Recommended Real-World Limits

Authoring an MPEG-2 stream at the absolute maximum peak of 9.8 Mbps is generally not recommended. While compliant hardware players should technically support it, pushed-to-the-limit bitrates frequently cause buffer underruns, stuttering, or playback failures on burned media (DVD-R/+R) and older standalone players.

For reliable playback across all devices, standard industry practice recommends capping the peak video bitrate between 8.0 Mbps and 8.5 Mbps when using compressed Dolby Digital (AC-3) audio.
