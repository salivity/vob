Can a VOB File Support 96 kHz Audio?

Yes, a VOB (Video Object) file can contain high-sample-rate audio streams sampled at 96 kHz. Although standard DVD-Video content typically uses 48 kHz audio, the DVD-Video specification explicitly supports uncompressed Linear PCM (LPCM) audio at sample rates of up to 96 kHz with bit depths of 16, 20, or 24 bits. This article explains how 96 kHz audio works within VOB containers, the technical limitations involved, and which formats support these high sample rates.

The Standard DVD-Video Specification and LPCM

VOB files are based on the MPEG-2 Program Stream format and serve as the core container for DVD-Video discs. While compressed formats like Dolby Digital (AC-3) and standard DTS are limited to 48 kHz on DVD-Video, the specification includes high-resolution Linear PCM (LPCM) audio tracks.

Under the DVD-Video standard, LPCM audio can be encoded with the following parameters:

  • Sample Rates: 48 kHz or 96 kHz
  • Bit Depths: 16-bit, 20-bit, or 24-bit
  • Channels: Up to 8 channels at 48 kHz, but restricted at 96 kHz

Channel and Bandwidth Limitations at 96 kHz

While 96 kHz audio is fully supported, bandwidth limitations constrain the maximum number of channels allowed inside a standard VOB container.

The maximum allowable audio bitrate for an LPCM stream on a DVD-Video disc is 6.144 Mbps, within an overall total stream limit (video, audio, and subtitles combined) of 10.08 Mbps. Because a 96 kHz, 24-bit audio stream requires roughly 4.6 Mbps for stereo, the format is restricted to two channels (stereo). You cannot run a 5.1 surround mix in uncompressed 96 kHz LPCM inside a standard DVD-Video VOB file due to these bitrate ceilings.

Formats That Do and Do Not Support 96 kHz in VOB

  • Supported (LPCM): Stereo 96 kHz LPCM is the primary method used for commercial high-resolution audio on DVD-Video, commonly found on music releases, concert discs, and Digital Audio Discs (DADs).
  • Unsupported (Standard AC-3): Dolby Digital in DVD-Video is fixed at a 48 kHz sampling rate.
  • Special Cases (DTS 96/24): DTS 96/24 is an extension of the core DTS format that allows 96 kHz, 24-bit multi-channel audio within standard DVD-Video VOB structures. Compatible players extract the core stream at 48 kHz and use an extension stream to reconstruct the full 96 kHz output.

Playback Compatibility

Virtually all modern software media players (such as VLC, MPC-HC, and mpv) can decode 96 kHz LPCM or DTS 96/24 audio streams directly from standalone VOB files. On standalone hardware DVD and Blu-ray players, playback depends on the hardware's internal digital-to-analog converter (DAC). If the player's hardware does not support direct 96 kHz output or copy-protection restrictions apply via digital outputs, the player will automatically downsample the audio to 48 kHz.
