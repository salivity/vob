Maximum Video Resolution for NTSC VOB Files

The maximum permissible video resolution for an NTSC VOB file is standard definition 720 × 480 pixels. This article explains the technical specifications governing VOB files within the DVD-Video standard, the role of pixel aspect ratios, and the strict encoding constraints that define NTSC playback.

The 720 × 480 Standard

VOB (Video Object) files are container formats based on the MPEG-2 Program Stream specification, explicitly designed for DVD-Video media. Under the official DVD Forum specifications for the NTSC television system, the highest supported video resolution—commonly known as "Full D1"—is 720 × 480 pixels.

Any file authored to compliant DVD-Video specifications cannot exceed this dimension. Attempting to encode a VOB file at high-definition resolutions (such as 1280 × 720 or 1920 × 1080) violates standard DVD authoring rules and will cause playback failure on standard DVD players.

Aspect Ratios and Non-Square Pixels

Although the maximum raw pixel dimensions are locked at 720 × 480, NTSC VOB files support two distinct display aspect ratios:

  • 4:3 Standard: Displays full-screen video using a non-square pixel aspect ratio of roughly 10:11 (0.91).
  • 16:9 Widescreen (Anamorphic): Horizontally compresses a widescreen picture into the same 720 × 480 grid using a pixel aspect ratio of approximately 40:33 (1.21). When played back on a compatible display, the player stretches the picture horizontally to match modern widescreen televisions without altering the underlying 720 × 480 frame.

Frame Rate and Scanning

An NTSC VOB file operating at 720 × 480 must adhere to a frame rate of 29.97 frames per second (fps), delivered as 59.94 interlaced fields per second. Content originally produced at 24 fps (such as theatrical film) is converted to the required 29.97 fps rate within the MPEG-2 stream using a process called 2:3 pulldown.

Permissible Lower NTSC Resolutions

While 720 × 480 represents the maximum limit, the DVD-Video standard also permits lower resolutions within NTSC VOB files for specific compression efficiency requirements:

  • 704 × 480: Commonly used in television broadcast transfers (4:3 or 16:9).
  • 352 × 480 (Half D1): Halves the horizontal resolution to conserve bit rate (4:3 only).
  • 352 × 240 (SIF): Uses MPEG-1 or MPEG-2 encoding matching standard VCD resolution (4:3 only).
