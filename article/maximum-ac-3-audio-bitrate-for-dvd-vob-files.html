Maximum AC-3 Audio Bitrate for DVD VOB Files

This article explains the maximum allowable audio bitrate for an AC-3 (Dolby Digital) stream multiplexed inside a standard VOB container. It covers the technical limitations defined by the DVD-Video specification, typical channel configurations, and the playback issues that arise if these limits are exceeded.

The Maximum AC-3 Bitrate Limit

The maximum allowable audio bitrate for an AC-3 audio track inside a standard DVD-compliant VOB file is 448 kbps (kilobits per second).

While the general Dolby Digital (ATSC A/52) standard supports bitrates up to 640 kbps, the official DVD-Video specification strictly limits AC-3 audio to a ceiling of 448 kbps. Any VOB file intended for standard DVD authoring and hardware playback must adhere to this 448 kbps ceiling to ensure compatibility.

Standard Bitrate Allocations by Channel Layout

Audio engineers typically allocate bitrates based on the number of audio channels in the AC-3 stream:

  • Mono (1.0): 96 kbps to 128 kbps
  • Stereo (2.0): 192 kbps to 224 kbps
  • Surround Sound (5.1): 384 kbps to 448 kbps

For full 5.1-channel surround sound, 448 kbps is universally considered the optimal quality setting on DVD-Video, delivering the maximum fidelity permitted by the specification.

Overall VOB Multiplex Constraints

In addition to the individual audio track limit, the VOB file is bound by a total multiplex rate constraint:

  • Maximum Total Bitrate: 10.08 Mbps (combining all video, audio, and subtitle streams).
  • Maximum Video Bitrate: 9.8 Mbps.

If an AC-3 audio track is encoded at 448 kbps, the maximum allowable bitrate for the accompanying video stream must be reduced accordingly to prevent the total multiplexed stream from exceeding the 10.08 Mbps limit.

Impact of Exceeding 448 kbps

Encoding an AC-3 stream at 640 kbps inside a VOB container violates DVD-Video specifications. While modern software media players on computers may decode non-standard VOB files containing 640 kbps AC-3 audio without issue, standalone hardware DVD players and set-top units will encounter buffer underruns. This results in audio dropouts, severe stuttering, out-of-sync playback, or complete playback failure.
