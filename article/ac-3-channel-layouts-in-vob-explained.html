AC-3 Channel Layouts in VOB Explained

The DVD-Video specification relies heavily on Dolby Digital (AC-3) as a primary, mandatory audio codec packaged inside VOB (Video Object) container files. AC-3 streams within VOB containers provide versatile audio delivery, scaling from single-channel mono up to full 5.1-channel surround sound. This article outlines the specific channel layout configurations defined by the AC-3 standard and supported by VOB files, including the underlying channel coding modes and the low-frequency effects (LFE) channel option.

Understanding AC-3 Channel Coding Modes

In the AC-3 bitstream format (standardized as ATSC A/52), channel configurations are defined by a 3-bit field known as acmod (Audio Coding Mode), paired with a 1-bit lfeon (Low-Frequency Effects on) flag. The acmod parameter specifies the number and spatial assignment of the main full-bandwidth channels, while the LFE bit indicates the presence of a dedicated subwoofer track (.1).

VOB containers adhere to these standard AC-3 definitions, allowing for eight primary channel arrangements, each optionally combinable with an LFE channel.

Supported Channel Configurations

1. 1/0 (1.0 - Mono)

  • Channels: Center (C)
  • Description: A single, full-bandwidth channel. In VOB playback, this mono track is typically routed equally to the front-left and front-right speakers or directly to the center speaker in multi-speaker setups.

2. 2/0 (2.0 - Stereo, Dolby Surround, or Dual Mono)

  • Channels: Left, Right (L, R)
  • Description: The most common two-channel configuration in VOB files. This mode supports:
    • Standard Stereo: Independent left and right audio tracks.
    • Dolby Surround (Lt/Rt): A matrix-encoded stereo track containing embedded center and surround information decodable by Dolby Pro Logic decoders.
    • Dual Mono (1+1): Two completely separate mono programs within the same stream (frequently used for bilingual commentary tracks on DVDs).

3. 3/0 (3.0 - 3-Channel Stereo)

  • Channels: Left, Center, Right (L, C, R)
  • Description: Provides three full-bandwidth front channels without surround channels, establishing a wide, stable front soundstage with dedicated dialogue anchoring in the center.

4. 2/1 (3.0 - Surround)

  • Channels: Left, Right, Surround (L, R, S)
  • Description: Features left and right front channels alongside a single, monophonic surround channel.

5. 3/1 (4.0 - Surround)

  • Channels: Left, Center, Right, Surround (L, C, R, S)
  • Description: Features three discrete front channels and one monophonic surround channel. This mirrors the channel arrangement produced by original analog matrixed Dolby Stereo cinema releases.

6. 2/2 (4.0 - Quadraphonic)

  • Channels: Left, Right, Left Surround, Right Surround (L, R, Ls, Rs)
  • Description: A four-channel configuration with stereo front channels and discrete stereo surround channels, omitting a center channel.

7. 3/2 (5.0 - Surround)

  • Channels: Left, Center, Right, Left Surround, Right Surround (L, C, R, Ls, Rs)
  • Description: Five full-bandwidth channels featuring a fully discrete front and rear soundstage without a dedicated subwoofer track.

The Low-Frequency Effects (LFE) Option

The lfeon flag adds a limited-bandwidth (3 Hz to 120 Hz) bass channel to any of the above configurations. While technically applicable to several modes, it is most commonly applied to:

  • 3/2 + LFE (5.1 Surround): The benchmark multi-channel audio format for DVD-Video VOB streams, consisting of Left, Center, Right, Left Surround, Right Surround, and an active LFE (.1) channel.
  • 2/0 + LFE (2.1 Stereo): Occasionally utilized for tracks requiring dedicated low-frequency bass output alongside stereo audio.

Summary of Common Practical Implementations

While the AC-3 specification allows all permutations within a VOB container, standard DVD-Video authoring overwhelmingly utilizes two primary configurations:

  1. 2.0 (2/0): Standard stereo or matrix-encoded Dolby Surround.
  2. 5.1 (3/2 with LFE): Full discrete six-channel surround sound.
