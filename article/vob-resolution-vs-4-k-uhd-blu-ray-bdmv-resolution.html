VOB Resolution vs 4K UHD Blu-ray BDMV Resolution

This article compares the maximum video resolution of standard DVD VOB files with that of modern 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray BDMV containers. While both file structures represent peak optical disc standards of their respective eras, their visual capabilities differ radically. Comparing the two reveals a dramatic evolution in digital video fidelity, moving from standard-definition broadcast standards to ultra-high-definition home theater specifications that feature over twenty times the pixel density.

VOB File Maximum Resolution

A VOB (Video Object) file is the core container format used on standard DVD-Video discs. Introduced in the late 1990s, VOB files are constrained by the strict limitations of the DVD-Video standard:

  • NTSC Standard: 720 × 480 pixels (interlaced or progressive at 29.97 or 23.976 frames per second)
  • PAL Standard: 720 × 576 pixels (interlaced or progressive at 25 frames per second)
  • Total Pixel Count: Approximately 345,600 pixels (NTSC) or 414,720 pixels (PAL)

VOB files utilize the legacy MPEG-2 codec. Widescreen 16:9 presentations on DVD do not increase pixel resolution; instead, they use anamorphic stretching, which digitally compresses a 16:9 picture into the standard 720-pixel-wide frame and stretches it horizontally during playback.

4K UHD Blu-ray BDMV Maximum Resolution

BDMV (Blu-ray Disc Movie) is the folder and file structure used on standard and Ultra HD Blu-ray discs. Inside a 4K UHD BDMV directory, video streams are encapsulated in M2TS containers built to the modern Ultra HD Blu-ray standard:

  • Maximum Resolution: 3840 × 2160 pixels (Ultra HD 4K)
  • Frame Rates: Supports up to 60 progressive frames per second (fps)
  • Total Pixel Count: 8,294,400 pixels

4K BDMV structures utilize the HEVC (H.265) compression format, which accommodates square pixels (1:1 pixel aspect ratio), rendering true native widescreen without requiring anamorphic distortion.

Direct Comparison

Feature VOB (DVD-Video) BDMV (4K UHD Blu-ray)
Max Resolution (Width × Height) 720 × 480 (NTSC) / 720 × 576 (PAL) 3840 × 2160
Total Megapixels ~0.35 MP (NTSC) / ~0.41 MP (PAL) ~8.29 MP
Pixel Density Multiplier 1x (Baseline) ~24x (vs. NTSC) / ~20x (vs. PAL)
Scan Type Interlaced (480i/576i) or Progressive Native Progressive (2160p)
Pixel Aspect Ratio Non-square (Anamorphic) Square (Native 1:1)
Primary Codec MPEG-2 HEVC / H.265

Impact on Image Clarity

A 4K UHD BDMV stream delivers approximately 24 times more raw visual information than an NTSC VOB file. In practical terms, this difference eliminates visible scan lines, eliminates compression artifacts caused by low bandwidth, and allows display on large modern screens without the heavy pixel interpolation and blur associated with upscaling legacy VOB video. Furthermore, while VOB is limited to an 8-bit standard dynamic range (Rec. 601 color space), 4K BDMV supports 10-bit high dynamic range (HDR10, Dolby Vision) paired with the expansive Rec. 2020 color gamut.
