Who Developed the VOB File Format?

The VOB (Video Object) file format was developed and standardized by the DVD Forum to serve as the core container format for consumer DVD-Video discs. As the backbone of the physical DVD revolution in the late 1990s, VOB allowed multiplexed digital video, multiple audio tracks, subtitles, and interactive menus to exist in a single, structured file. This article examines the organization responsible for creating the format, how it was standardized, and the technical framework that enabled widespread consumer video distribution.

The Developers: The DVD Consortium and DVD Forum

The VOB format was not invented by a single individual or company; rather, it was developed by the DVD Consortium, an international working group formed in 1995. The consortium was founded by ten major technology and entertainment corporations:

  • Toshiba
  • Sony
  • Philips
  • Matsushita Electric Industrial (now Panasonic)
  • Hitachi
  • Pioneer
  • Thomson
  • JVC
  • Mitsubishi Electric
  • Time Warner

In 1997, the DVD Consortium officially renamed itself the DVD Forum, opening its membership to other hardware manufacturers, software developers, and media companies to promote and maintain standardized specifications for read-only optical discs.

Standardization in the DVD-Video Specification

To prevent a format war similar to the Betamax versus VHS conflict, the founding companies unified competing proposals—primarily Toshiba's Super Density (SD) disc and Sony/Philips' Multimedia Compact Disc (MMCD).

The technical specifications for the VOB format were formalized within the DVD Specifications for Read-Only Disc, Part 3: Video Specifications (often referred to as the "DVD-Video Book").

Under this standard, VOB is technically a subset of the MPEG-2 Program Stream (ISO/IEC 13818-1), but with specific, mandatory constraints designed to ensure compatibility across all consumer standalone DVD players:

  • Video: Restricted to standard-definition MPEG-2 or MPEG-1 video streams with strictly defined bitrates, frame rates, and resolutions (such as 720×480 for NTSC and 720×576 for PAL).
  • Audio: Support for multiple interleaved audio tracks, including Dolby Digital (AC-3), DTS, Linear PCM (LPCM), and MPEG-1 Audio Layer II.
  • Sub-pictures and Navigation: Capability to multiplex up to 32 subtitle/overlay streams (sub-pictures) and real-time navigation commands.
  • File Size Limits: Files are divided into chunks no larger than 1 gigabyte (1,073,741,824 bytes) to ensure compatibility across older operating systems and file structures, primarily the Universal Disk Format (UDF) MicroUDF bridge.

Role in the VIDEO_TS Structure

Within the standardized DVD directory architecture, VOB files reside in the VIDEO_TS folder and work in conjunction with two companion file types:

  • IFO (Information) files: Contain disc navigation data, aspect ratios, audio channel configurations, and chapter markers.
  • BUP (Backup) files: Exact duplicates of the IFO files to ensure playback if the primary data sectors are damaged.

By standardizing VOB as a uniform container strictly tied to MPEG-2 compression, the DVD Forum established the dominant physical media standard for consumer video distribution from 1996 through the mid-2000s.
