Can Modern Consoles Play VOB Files From USB?

Modern gaming consoles generally lack native, out-of-the-box support for loose VOB (Video Object) files stored on an external USB drive, but direct playback is still achievable on select platforms. While Sony's PlayStation 5 and the Nintendo Switch cannot play these legacy DVD files directly from external storage, Microsoft's Xbox One and Xbox Series X|S consoles can play them natively via downloadable media player applications. For systems that do not support the format, file conversion remains the most reliable solution.

Xbox Series X|S and Xbox One Compatibility

Xbox consoles cannot play VOB files through the default "Media Player" or "Movies & TV" apps because the platform lacks the native container support for raw DVD formats. However, because the Xbox ecosystem supports the Universal Windows Platform (UWP), users can download VLC for Windows or Kodi directly from the Microsoft Store on the console.

Once installed, VLC can directly detect an external USB drive (formatted in NTFS or FAT32) and play loose .vob files without conversion. VLC carries its own internal decoders for MPEG-2 video and AC-3 audio, bypassing the console's native playback limitations.

PlayStation 4 and PlayStation 5 Compatibility

Neither the PlayStation 4 nor the PlayStation 5 can directly play loose VOB files from a USB drive. The native PS5 Media Gallery and PS4 Media Player support modern containers such as MP4, MKV, and WebM, but they strictly ignore the .vob extension.

Renaming the file extension from .vob to .mpg or .mp4 occasionally bypasses the file filter, but playback often results in errors. The console's operating system frequently fails to decode the multiplexed AC-3 (Dolby Digital) or DTS audio tracks commonly bundled inside VOB containers. Because the PlayStation Network does not offer standalone players like VLC, direct local USB playback of raw VOB files is not currently possible.

Nintendo Switch Compatibility

The Nintendo Switch does not support external media playback. The system's USB-C port does not interface with external hard drives or USB thumb drives for personal media, making VOB playback impossible without unauthorized system modifications.

The Recommended Workaround: Remuxing or Transcoding

If using an Xbox with VLC is not an option, the fastest way to play your media is by converting the VOB files into a modern container.

  • Remuxing (Fastest): Tools like MKVToolNix can extract the MPEG-2 video and audio streams from the VOB wrapper and place them inside an .mkv container in seconds without re-encoding the video quality.
  • Transcoding (Best Compatibility): Software like HandBrake can transcode the VOB files into an H.264 or H.265 .mp4 file, ensuring universal compatibility with the built-in USB media players on both PlayStation and Xbox consoles.
