Maximum DTS Audio Bitrate in a VOB Container

This article outlines the bitrate constraints for Digital Theater Systems (DTS) audio tracks housed within the DVD-Video VOB (Video Object) container. Under official DVD-Video specifications, the maximum allowable bitrate for a DTS audio stream inside a VOB file is 1,509.75 kbps—commonly referred to as the full-rate 1,536 kbps DTS stream.

DTS Bitrate Tiers in VOB Containers

The DVD-Video standard specifies two distinct bitrate tiers for coherent DTS audio streams:

  • Full Bitrate (Maximum): 1,509.75 kbps (often identified as 1.5 Mbps or nominal 1,536 kbps).
  • Half Bitrate: 754.5 kbps (nominal 768 kbps).

While the standard core DTS protocol outside of DVD can theoretically handle variations, any DTS audio stream authored into a compliant DVD-Video VOB file must strictly conform to one of these two operational rates.

Buffer and Multiplexing Limits

A VOB container functions as an MPEG-2 Program Stream with specialized private streams designated for DTS packets (Private Stream 1, substream 0x88 to 0x8F). When multiplexing a full-bitrate DTS track into a VOB container, the total bit budget must adhere to the overall DVD constraints:

  • Maximum Total Bitrate: The combined bitstream for all video, audio, and subtitle streams cannot exceed 10.08 Mbps.
  • Maximum Video Bitrate: Typically throttled to around 8.0 to 8.5 Mbps when using a 1,509.75 kbps DTS track to prevent buffer underruns in hardware decoders.

Because a full-bitrate DTS track consumes roughly 15% of the entire allowable multiplex capacity, many commercial DVD releases opted for the half-bitrate (754.5 kbps) format to preserve bandwidth for higher video quality.

Sampling and Channel Specifications

For a DTS track to remain valid at the maximum bitrate in a VOB file, it must also meet the standard DVD audio structural parameters:

  • Sampling Rate: Strictly 48 kHz.
  • Channel Configurations: Up to 5.1 discrete surround sound channels (5 main channels plus 1 Low-Frequency Effects channel).
  • Word Length: 16-bit to 24-bit source material encoded via the DTS Coherent Acoustics core algorithm.
